## 1. THE OVEN

- CasaKoa is Pakistani-made, dual-fuel gas burner, brick floor, up to ~500°C. Same category as AVPN spec (485°C, 60–90 sec bake).
- **Preheat:** 25–30 minutes minimum on full gas. The brick must be heat-saturated, not just the air.
- **Target floor temp:** 380–400°C (NOT higher — too hot burns the bottom before the top cooks).
- **Target dome/air temp:** 450–500°C.
- **Between pizzas:** 2–3 minutes for the stone to recover heat.
- **Bake:** 60–90 seconds, turn 180° at the 30–40 second mark.
- **Doming:** in the last 10–15 seconds, lift the pizza closer to the dome/flame with the peel to finish the top and char the cornicione without burning the base.
- **Equipment gap:** an infrared thermometer gun (Daraz, Rs. 1,500–3,000) is the single most important accessory. Measure the *floor*, not the air.

| "Jo aata" (Ama brand, barley flour) | **Rejected.** Barley has essentially no gluten. Would produce crumbly, untearable dough. |
| Chakki atta + maida blend | **ADOPTED.** From an r/pakistan comment (2:5 ratio). Softened to ~20% atta. |

### Key finding
**Toss uses plain maida.** Confirmed directly by their chef. So world-class pizza in this oven is achievable without 00 flour.

### Why maida behaves differently
- Maida is finely milled but has **weak, soft gluten**. Italian 00 has strong AND extensible gluten — that's what makes Vito's dough look silky and slime-like in videos.
- **Dawood's dough will never look as smooth as Vito's.** This is the flour, not his technique, not his stand mixer, not salt levels, not mixing order. Confirmed repeatedly.
- Maida also **hydrates slowly**, which is why dough seems sticky/strandy longer than expected.
- Maida is high-starch, low-protein — starch browns faster, which contributes to burning.

### Current flour blend (per 1kg total)
- 250g maida (in the poolish)
- 550g maida (in the final dough)
- 200g **fine** chakki atta

**CRITICAL:** Use the *fine/safaid* chakki atta (smooth, pale, no visible bran flakes), NOT the coarse whole wheat one. He has both. The coarse one makes a dense, gritty, tearing dough.

### Still worth pursuing
- Ask Toss / Islamabad pizzerias which maida brand and mill they use. Commercial brands (Sunridge, Golden Harvest) may have higher protein than loose maida.
- Restaurant supply wholesalers in I-9 Industrial Area or Rawalpindi wholesale markets may carry specialty/high-protein flours that don't reach retail.
- If anyone travels abroad — have them bring back Caputo. Even one bag, just to do a side-by-side and feel the difference.

## 3. THE DOUGH — CURRENT WORKING RECIPE

Method: **Vito Iacopelli poolish**, adapted for maida and Islamabad's climate.

### Poolish (make first)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Water | 250ml |
| Maida | 250g |
| Instant dry yeast (Saf-Instant) | see table below |
| Honey | 5g (1 tsp) |

**Yeast + water temp by timeline:**

| Timeline | Yeast | Water temp | Counter time | Fridge time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Same-day / fast (~6–7h total) | 7g | Room temp (~25°C) | 2–3h until peaked, no fridge | — |
| Vito standard (16–24h) | 5g | Room temp | 1.5–2h | 16–20h |
| Long / overnight in hot weather | 3g | **Cold 8–10°C** | 1.5h | 20–24h |

**Method:** dissolve honey + yeast in the water → add maida → mix until no dry spots (thick wet batter, not a dough) → cover **loosely** on the counter → then seal tight and fridge.

**Ratio rule:** 250g poolish is designed for **1kg total flour** (25% pre-fermented). Can be stretched to 1.5kg total flour max (~17%). Beyond that the poolish stops mattering — make a second poolish instead.

**What a ready poolish looks like:** heavily bubbled, possibly large dome bubbles on the surface, domed or *just* starting to collapse in the center, smells pleasantly yeasty/slightly tangy. A slightly collapsed poolish is at *peak flavor* — that's ideal, not a failure.

Brown spots/specks on the surface are normal (undissolved flour or settled honey).

Rest 20–30 minutes on the counter before mixing into the final dough.

### Final dough (per 1kg total flour)

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| All of the poolish | — |
| Maida | 550g |
| Fine chakki atta | 200g |
| Ice-cold water | **380ml (65%)** or **450ml (70%)** |
| Fine pink salt | 35g |
| Olive oil | 4 tbsp |

Water should be genuinely cold — fridge water, or tap water with ice cubes melted in and any remaining ice removed. In current Islamabad heat this matters more than ever: kneading generates friction heat, and warm dough overproofs fast.

### Stand mixer method (step by step) — revised 2026-09-07

Revised after a batch that stayed liquid and never gathered on the hook. Root causes: kneading on speed 1 (which only stirs on a home mixer) and too much water in the bowl from the start for weak maida. Research and sources: `research/mixing-and-kneading.md`.

1. **Water first.** Half of the water into the bowl (190 ml at 65%). Dissolve the 35 g salt in the other half and keep it aside.
2. Add all the poolish, then both flours on top.
3. Dough hook. **Lowest speed for 2–3 min**, only until no dry flour is left: a rough, tight ball (about 45–50% hydration at this point), not a batter.
4. **Rest 15–20 min covered.** Maida drinks slowly; this is where it catches up.
5. **Kneading speed for 6–8 min.** On Dawood's mixer that is **speed 5–6** (observed 2026-09-07: speed 2 barely stirs and the dough only gathered from the walls at 5–6). Kneading speed is the lowest setting where the hook pushes the dough round the bowl and it gathers within a minute; a KitchenAid does this on 2. Knead until the dough gathers on the hook and pulls clean from the bowl.
6. **Second water (bassinage):** the salty half in 3–4 small additions at the same kneading speed. Wait until each addition has vanished and the dough gathers on the hook again before the next. 8–10 min. Too fast and it turns to soup.
7. Olive oil last, 2 min.
8. Stop when the dough is smooth and satiny, even if it fails the windowpane: for an overnight or cold-fermented dough, fermentation finishes the gluten. Total under 40 min.
9. Check: clinging to the hook, bowl clean, tacky not gluey. A hook spinning through sloppy dough that never gathers = too much water too early, or too little dough for the bowl (scrape down, hold water back, 5 more min).
10. Rest 15–30 minutes covered, then ball at 280 g and proof.

The old "never above speed 2" rule was a KitchenAid rule and does not apply to this mixer. Respect the manual's maximum for dough if it states one, and stop if the bowl flexes or the motor labours. Still true: **never add flour** to fix stickiness.

### THE MOST IMPORTANT TROUBLESHOOTING RULE

**When the dough goes strandy, breaking, or won't come together — STOP KNEADING.**

More kneading tears the fragile gluten strands. Instead:
- Cover with a damp towel. Walk away **20 minutes**.
- Knead 5 minutes on low.
- If still not there: rest 15–20 min, knead 5 min. Repeat 2–3 cycles.

This is passive autolyse — the maida finishes absorbing water and gluten strands bond on their own. This works; it was used successfully multiple times.

Dough temperature: the mixer adds roughly 12–17 °C over a full knead. In a 30 °C kitchen the water must be near 0 °C, and when the maths asks for less than that, replace 20% of the water with crushed ice (by weight) added with the first water. If the dough comes out above 27 °C, stop the mixer and finish with three sets of stretch-and-folds 15 minutes apart.

**Never add more flour to fix stickiness.**

### Salt notes
- 35g per 1kg flour (3.5%) — higher end, deliberately. Adds flavor and tightens/strengthens gluten, reducing stickiness.
- **Add salt AFTER 3–4 minutes of initial mixing**, not at the very start. Early salt inhibits initial gluten formation.

### Balling
- Oil the counter and hands. **Never flour** for this step — flour creates dry clumps in wet dough.
- Divide at 280g with an oiled scraper/knife and a scale.
- Keep the top side always on top. Tuck edges underneath while rotating to build surface tension. Smooth top, seam underneath.
- Place on an oiled tray with 5cm+ between balls. Cover with cling wrap **oiled on the underside** so rising balls don't stick.

### Proofing (WARM WEATHER — late March, ~28°C Islamabad)

| | Cooler weather | Now (~28°C) |
|---|---|---|
| Ball proof at room temp | 4–5 hours | **3–3.5 hours** |
| Poolish counter time | 2h | 1.5h |
| Overproofing risk | Moderate | **High — watch closely** |

**Poke test:** press a floured finger gently into a ball.
- Springs back **slowly and only halfway** → ready.
- Springs back **fast** → needs more time.
- Indent **stays completely** → slightly overproofed. Still usable, handle gently, expect less oven spring.

Start checking at 2.5 hours in current weather.

### Fridge temperature effects
| Fridge temp | Effect |
|---|---|
| Below 2°C | Yeast nearly dormant. No overproofing risk but less flavor. Dough stiffer; needs 4–5h to proof after balling. |
| **3–5°C (ideal)** | Slow fermentation, good flavor, no overproofing. |
| Above 7–8°C | Yeast stays active. Overproofing risk: slack, sticky, boozy smell, liquid on surface, flat cornicione. |

**Reading the dough after cold ferment:**
- Doubled, mildly tangy smell, firm and elastic → perfect.
- Tripled, boozy/sour, slack and sticky, surface liquid → overproofed. Usable but handle gently, expect a flatter result.
- Barely risen, very stiff → fridge too cold. Give a full 5h room-temp proof.

Large dome bubbles on the surface after cold ferment are **normal and good** — active fermentation.

### Cling wrap
**No holes.** Sealed tight, pressed close to the dough surface. Holes let in dry air and form a skin, which creates tough spots when folded in during balling. Enough gas escapes at the bowl rim naturally.

### Timeline planning — three validated arrangements

**A. Same-day (~6–7 hours total)**
```
Now        Poolish (7g yeast, room temp water), counter
+2.5–3h    Poolish peaks. Make dough. Ball immediately.
+3–3.5h    Balls proofed. Light oven.
+30min     Pizza.
```

**B. Vito standard (overnight poolish, same-day dough) — RECOMMENDED FOR EVENTS**
```
Day 1 ~3pm   Poolish (5g yeast). 1.5–2h counter, then fridge.
Day 2 9am    Poolish out. 20–30 min rest.
Day 2 9:30   Make dough. Ball immediately.
Day 2 10am   Balls proofing at room temp.
Day 2 12:30  Light CasaKoa.
Day 2 1pm    Pizza.
```
Lower risk, proven, simple. **This is the recommended default.**

**C. Split method (overnight poolish + overnight bulk cold ferment)**
```
Day 1 evening   Poolish (3g yeast, cold water). 1.5h counter, fridge.
Day 2 evening   Make dough. One bulk mass in oiled bowl. Fridge overnight.
Day 3 morning   Ball while cold. 3–3.5h room temp proof.
Day 3 afternoon Pizza.
```
Technically ~10–15% more flavor complexity (cold ferment favors lactic acid = creamy mild tang; enzymes convert starch to sugar; gluten fully relaxes). **But higher overproofing risk.** Save for low-pressure experimentation, not events.

**Note on cold-ferment shortcuts that DON'T work:** you cannot skip the room-temperature ball proof. Cold dough from the fridge is tight and dense, springs back, tears, and yields a flat cornicione. Minimum 2 hours, realistically 3–4.

### Storing balled dough
Individual balls ferment slower than a bulk mass. Once balled, they hold in the fridge another 18–24 hours without overproofing. Useful for splitting a batch across two days: ball everything, keep some out to proof, put the rest back in the fridge on an oiled covered tray.

## 4. SHAPING

- Dust with a **50/50 blend of fine sooji (semolina) and maida**. This is Vito's method and it works: sooji granules act like ball bearings and resist burning; maida fills the gaps for smooth coverage. Use it on the work surface, on the dough balls, and on the peel.
- **Never use a rolling pin.** It crushes the gas bubbles the fermentation built.
- Press from the center outward with fingertips, leaving a 1–2cm rim untouched (the cornicione).
- Drape over the knuckles and rotate, letting gravity stretch it. Or use the Neapolitan slap technique.
- **The center must be 2–3mm — nearly translucent.** This was a confirmed problem: his pizzas came out with a great crust but a thick, heavy center. He wasn't stretching the middle thin enough.
- If 280g balls are producing thick 8–9 inch pizzas, either stretch wider (10–12 inches) or drop ball weight to 220–240g.
- Once the dough is on the peel you have ~20–30 seconds before it sticks. Top fast, launch fast.

## 5. THE SAUCE — WAS THE BIGGEST FAILURE, NOW FIXED

### What went wrong originally
The Toss chef made the sauce by heating the tomatoes, peeling, **blending**, and **cooking it down twice**. Result on Dawood's pizza: weak flavor, no punch in the first bite, and it turned **orange** after baking instead of staying red.

**Diagnosis:**
- **Cooking destroys flavor.** Fresh tomato flavor comes from volatile aromatics that evaporate with heat. A raw sauce is bright and vibrant; a cooked one is flat and generic.
- **Blending causes the orange color.** It emulsifies air and water into the pigment. Cooking lightens it further.
- **Reducing is pointless here.** At 500°C for 60–90 seconds the oven evaporates moisture instantly. Pre-reducing just means double-cooked, lifeless tomatoes.

### The correct method
1. Open the can.
2. **DRAIN the packing juice off first.** (He initially skipped this and got a thin, watery, orange liquid. If already mixed, strain through a sieve for 5 minutes and keep the thick pulp.)
3. Crush the solid tomatoes **by hand**, squeezing through the fingers. Chunky and uneven — not smooth.
4. Remove any hard white cores.
5. Add ~½ tsp fine pink salt. Taste — should be bright, sweet, tomatoey. Another pinch if flat.
6. **Do not cook it. Do not blend it. No garlic, onion, or herbs in the sauce.**

The sauce cooks on the pizza in 60–90 seconds and stays red.

### Tomatoes
- **Mara Italian Whole Peeled Tomatoes (Pomodori Pelati)** — Rs. 435 / 400g tin, **Al-Fatah**. 100% Italian product, halal certified, no added sugar. Two label variants exist (blue can and darker "in succo di pomodoro" can) — **same product**.
- Note: the tomatoes inside are round rather than elongated plum/San Marzano shape. Still a legitimate Italian pelati, works fine.
- **Yield:** one drained 400g can ≈ 240–260g thick sauce ≈ **4–5 pizzas** at 50–60g each. Buy 3 cans for an event.
- Leftover crushed sauce keeps 3–4 days sealed in the fridge.
- **DOP San Marzano** was never located locally. Heirloom Farms (heirloomfarms.pk) grows fresh San Marzano variety tomatoes seasonally — worth revisiting.
- **Fresh tomato sauce (alternative):** score an X on the bottom, blanch 30 sec, ice bath, peel, deseed, crush by hand with salt. Brighter but more watery.
- He also has hydroponic red tomatoes and cherry tomatoes. **Cherry tomatoes are best used as a topping** (halved, they burst and caramelize into intense sweetness in the oven), not as sauce.
- **Never use tomato paste, purée, or passata as the base.**

### CONFIRMED PROBLEM: buffalo mozzarella went plasticky/rubbery

**Symptom:** cheese was hard to tear, not creamy, and "stayed in the mouth too long."

**Cause:** buffalo mozzarella has significantly more moisture and fat than fior di latte. At 500°C the surface moisture boils off in seconds, the milk proteins seize around the fat, and it turns rubbery. **The window between perfectly melted and rubbery is about 10–15 seconds.**

**Fixes, in order of effectiveness:**
1. **BEST — don't bake the bufala at all.** Bake with fior di latte (or a small amount of another melting cheese) and tear **cold buffalo mozzarella over the pizza after it comes out**. Residual heat softens it to perfect creaminess. This is what high-end pizzerias do with bufala.
2. Add the cheese mid-bake: launch with just sauce + oil, then at 30–40 seconds drop the mozzarella on. Halves the heat exposure.
3. Pull the pizza the *moment* the cheese is melted and glossy with the first bubbles. Do not wait for browning on the cheese.
4. Drain harder: slice 3–4mm, press between double paper towels **25–30 minutes** (longer than fior di latte needs), gently squeeze.
5. Use less: **70–80g per pizza** for bufala.

### Cheese quantity
- Standard AVPN guidance: 80–100g/pizza.
- **Toss appears to use more** — Dawood observed a more generous, even, consistent layer. For fior di latte, **120g/pizza** distributed evenly with no bare patches is closer to the Toss experience.
- For bufala: 70–80g.
- Thin scattered cheese dries out faster; a generous even layer insulates itself and stays creamier.

### Draining
- Slice into 3–5mm rounds or tear into chunks (tearing is more traditional).
- Double layer of paper towels below, another on top.
- 15–20 min for fior di latte, 25–30 min for bufala.
- **Non-negotiable.** Skipping this = soggy center.

### Storage (researched)

**Fresh mozzarella cannot be stockpiled for weeks. It is not that kind of cheese.**

| Situation | Shelf life |
|---|---|
| **Unopened sealed pouch**, back of fridge at ≤4°C | 2–3 weeks from manufacture (check the date on the pouch) |
| Opened, submerged in brine, sealed container | 5–7 days max |
| Opened, no brine, just wrapped | 2–3 days |
| Frozen (sliced, single layer, then bagged) | 2–3 months — **melting use only**, texture becomes crumbly |

**Homemade brine:** 1 tsp salt per 1 cup cold filtered water. Submerge completely in an airtight container. **Change the brine every 2 days.**

**Storage rules:** back of the fridge, never the door (door temp fluctuates). Keep pouches flat, don't stack weight on them. Never freeze fresh mozzarella intended to be eaten fresh.

**Strategy: keep individual pouches sealed and only open what's needed on pizza day.** Order fresh per session rather than stockpiling. Burrata especially — order day-of or day-before only.

### Burrata
- **Never bake it.** Place the whole ball on the *finished* pizza straight out of the oven and tear it open so the stracciatella oozes.
- Eat within ~2 minutes or it soaks in and goes soggy.
- One burrata per pizza.
- Highly perishable — 1–2 days from purchase.

### Finishing cheese
- **Parmigiano Reggiano** — grated fresh over the pizza right after it comes out. Adds the umami that makes mozzarella-only pizza taste flat. **He did not have this for the first tests and it was a confirmed contributor to the "not enough goodness" problem.**
- Grana Padano — cheaper close cousin, equally good here.
- Pecorino Romano — sharper, saltier alternative.
- Source: Esajee's, Al-Fatah.

### Other cheeses to explore
- **Gorgonzola + honey** (see §8) — bianca base, no tomato.
- Ricotta — dollops before baking.
- Provolone — smoky, good with pepperoni.

## 7. TOPPINGS

### Beef bacon — Almasa, Rs. 3,800 / 500g

**Problem:** tasted great on its own but flat and unremarkable on the pizza.

**Cause:** it went on raw. At 500°C for 60–90 seconds it heats but never renders — the fat stays soft, the meat steams instead of crisping, and the flavor stays locked inside.

**Fix (full method):**
1. Chop into rough 2–3cm pieces.
2. Season: pinch of black pepper + pinch of smoked paprika + **½ tsp honey for the whole batch**. This creates a sweet-smoky glaze as it renders. This is the "why does theirs taste better" step.
3. Dry pan (no oil), very hot. 3–4 minutes, tossing, until edges crisp and fat is rendered. **Take it to ~70% done** — it finishes in the oven.
4. Rest on paper towels.
5. **KEEP THE RENDERED FAT.** Pour it into a small bowl. Drizzle a little over the pizza along with the olive oil, or brush a thin layer on the dough before the sauce. This pushes bacon flavor through the whole pizza instead of leaving it in isolated pieces.

### Mushrooms

**Key insight:** the dark, almost-black mushrooms on Toss's "pancetta" pizza are almost certainly just **white button mushrooms that have been properly sautéed.** Raw button mushrooms are pale; cooked down they shrink by half and turn deep brown.

**Method:** slice 3–4mm. Wide pan, screaming hot, good glug of olive oil. **Single layer — don't crowd** or they steam. Don't touch for 2–3 minutes, let one side brown. Flip, another 2–3 minutes. **Salt at the END** (early salt draws water and prevents browning). Total 5–8 min until shrunken, dark, crispy-edged. Optional: knob of butter + crushed garlic in the last minute.

**Fresh sources:** Metro Cash & Carry (most reliable), Carrefour, Al-Fatah, Itwar Bazaar (F-9/G-9), Sabzi Mandi. **Kohsar Market has poor fresh produce selection** — that's why he couldn't find them there.

**Canned (Fresco "Choice Whole"):** workable but inferior — pre-cooked, waterlogged, low flavor. If using: drain completely, pat dry, sauté 2 min to drive off moisture.

**Dried shiitake** (vacuum packs seen at the store): different product entirely. Needs 20–30 min rehydration, much stronger earthy flavor. Not for Neapolitan pizza.

### Pepperoni
Beef pepperoni (pork unavailable). Slice thin so it cups and crisps. Leave space between slices. Sources: Esajee's, Meat One.

### "Mortadella" from Kings — REJECTED
Square-shaped processed meat loaf, essentially Pakistani luncheon meat mislabeled. Real mortadella is a large round cylinder, pink with white fat studs and sometimes pistachio, fully cured, eaten cold and paper-thin. The Kings product is bland and rubbery — not worth using on pizza. If genuine imported Italian mortadella ever appears at Esajee's deli counter, that's worth buying (mortadella + burrata + pistachio is a great pizza, added post-bake).

### Also in inventory
Chicken sausages (slice into 5mm rounds), cherry tomatoes, fresh basil.

### Topping order — ALWAYS
```
Sauce → mozzarella → toppings → olive oil → BAKE → post-bake garnishes
```
Post-bake only: fresh basil, arugula, burrata, cold bufala, Parmigiano, prosciutto, cracked pepper, flaky salt, honey drizzle.

**Never put fresh herbs or delicate greens in the oven — they burn instantly at 500°C.**

### Don't overload
The thin Neapolitan center can't carry weight. A few pieces of each with breathing room beats piling on.

## 8. THE PIZZA MENU

**1. Margherita** — sauce, fior di latte, olive oil. Bake. Post-bake: torn basil, olive oil, grated Parmigiano. *Master this first.*

**2. Marinara** (no cheese) — sauce (slightly more generous), thin-sliced garlic, dried oregano, generous olive oil. Bake. More olive oil after. **This is the pure dough-and-sauce test — if this tastes great, everything else will.**

**3. Pancetta & Mushroom (the Toss recreation)** — sauce, mozzarella, pre-rendered seasoned beef bacon, sautéed dark mushrooms. Drizzle olive oil **plus a little reserved bacon fat**. Bake. Post-bake: cracked black pepper, Parmigiano, optional arugula + lemon squeeze.

**4. Pepperoni with Spicy Honey** — sauce, mozzarella, spaced pepperoni slices, olive oil. Bake. **Immediately drizzle spicy honey in a zigzag.** Optional chili flakes. *Predicted to be the crowd favorite.*

**5. Burrata & Cherry Tomato (the showstopper)** — thin film of sauce, **less** mozzarella (50–60g), 8–10 cherry tomato halves cut-side-up, generous olive oil. Bake (tomatoes burst and caramelize = natural sweetness, no sugar needed). Out of the oven: place a whole burrata in the center, tear it open, torn basil, best olive oil, cracked pepper, pinch of flaky salt. **Make this LAST — burrata waits for nobody.**

**6. Gorgonzola & Honey (bianca)** — no tomato. Mozzarella base + 30–40g crumbled gorgonzola (go light, it's intense). Bake. **Drizzle raw honey in a zigzag the moment it's out.** Optional: crushed walnuts, arugula. Divisive but memorable. Danish blue works if gorgonzola isn't available. Pakistani acacia or sidr honey is excellent here.

**7. Chicken Sausage & Mushroom** — sauce, mozzarella, sausage rounds, sautéed mushrooms, olive oil, dried oregano.

**8. Meat Lovers** — sauce, mozzarella, light amounts of pepperoni + bacon + sausage. Restraint is the point.

## 9. DIPPING SAUCES (for the leftover crust — important for a Pakistani crowd)

### Roasted Garlic & Herb Butter
Slice the top off a whole head of garlic, drizzle olive oil, wrap in foil, roast in the CasaKoa during preheat (or 180°C conventional for 40 min). Squeeze the soft cloves into a pot with:

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Butter | 100g |
| Roasted garlic | 1 full head |
| Pink salt | small pinch |
| Chili flakes | small pinch |
| Lemon juice | ½ lemon |
| Parsley (dried or fresh) | 1 tsp |
| Olive oil | 1 tbsp |

Melt on lowest heat, don't boil. The olive oil stops it solidifying too fast. Keep warm during service. Can be made a day ahead and gently reheated. **Double the batch for 6+ people.**

### Spicy Honey
Warm 3–4 tbsp honey on lowest heat 1 minute, stir in chili flakes (start ½ tsp, taste, adjust). Also used as a post-bake drizzle on pepperoni and bacon pizzas. Dead simple, huge impact.

### Yogurt Dip (leans into the Pakistani palate)
Thick strained/hung yogurt + 1 crushed garlic clove + salt + lemon squeeze + olive oil + dried mint or fresh coriander. Essentially an elevated raita. Cool tangy creaminess against hot charred crust.

**Recommendation: put out all three.** Turns the setup into a proper pizza bar.

## 10. OTHER INGREDIENTS

### Olive oil — CONFIRMED AVAILABLE IN STORE
**Mueloliva Extra Virgin.** Physically stocked at **Metro, Carrefour, Al-Fatah (Centaurus F-8, Bahria Town Rawalpindi), Esajee's.** No delivery needed.

**Must be EXTRA VIRGIN.** Mueloliva also sells "Extra Light" (refined, no flavor, for frying — useless here) and "Pomace" (lowest grade, solvent-extracted — never). Borges Extra Virgin is an equally available backup.

**Missing olive oil was a confirmed cause of the "not enough goodness in the first bite" problem.** Fat carries flavor — without it everything tastes thin. Use generously: drizzle before baking AND after.

### Salt
**Himalayan pink salt is completely fine** — mined at Khewra, an hour from Islamabad. Functionally identical to sea salt for dough and pizza.

Where salt appears:
- **Dough:** 35g per 1kg flour (added after 3–4 min of mixing)
- **Sauce:** generous pinch when crushing
- **Finishing:** tiny pinch of coarse/flaky salt on the baked pizza (optional but elevates)

### Pepper and seasoning
Neapolitan pizza uses almost no seasoning. Black pepper is a **post-bake** garnish only — great on burrata and on the sausage/mushroom pizza, not on a Margherita, never in the sauce or dough. **Dried oregano only goes on Marinara** (before baking). Chili flakes post-bake on pepperoni/bacon pizzas.

### Yeast
**Saf-Instant dry yeast** from any general store. This is what Toss uses ("packet yeast").

**"Brewer's yeast" searches return supplement capsules — that's deactivated nutritional yeast, a completely different product. Ignore it.** For real fresh yeast, ask any local naan/roti bakery for **khameer (خمیر)** — they buy it in bulk. Keep wrapped in the fridge, use within a week.

**Measuring 1g:** use the 0.1g precision scale (Daraz, Rs. 500–1,500). Tare a spoon or paper, tap out to 1.0g. Manual fallback: **one level ¼ teaspoon ≈ 1g** instant dry yeast, leveled flat with a knife.

### Semolina (sooji)
For dusting, in a 50/50 blend with maida. Fine sooji (the halwa grind), not coarse daliya. Available everywhere.

### Basil
Grows well in Islamabad — buy a plant from a nursery around F-6/F-7 Super Market. Also at Esajee's, Carrefour, Metro. Always added post-bake or in the last 5 seconds.

## 11. WHY THE FIRST PIZZAS UNDERWHELMED — ROOT CAUSE SUMMARY

Dawood's feedback after round one: *"didn't get a nice sauce taste… became orange… didn't feel like a lot of flavor… not a lot of goodness in the mouth."*

Ranked causes:
1. **Cooked + blended sauce** → killed brightness, turned it orange. *(Fix: raw hand-crushed, drained.)*
2. **No olive oil** → no fat to carry flavor, no richness, thin mouthfeel, dull crust. *(Fix: Mueloliva EVOO, generously, before and after.)*
3. **No Parmigiano** → no umami to amplify the savory notes. *(Fix: grate over post-bake.)*
4. **Raw un-rendered bacon** → flavor locked inside, no crisp, no rendered fat in the pizza.
5. **Canned un-sautéed mushrooms** → watery, bland, no caramelization.
6. **Overcooked buffalo mozzarella** → rubbery, plasticky, lingering in the mouth.
7. **Center stretched too thick** → heavy, bready, wrong texture ratio.
8. **Sugar in the Toss chef's dough recipe** → caramelizes and burns fast at 500°C. *(Removed. Zero sugar in the current recipe; the poolish's 5g honey is only there to feed the yeast.)*

## 16. CORE PRINCIPLES TO REMEMBER

- The sauce is **raw**. Always.
- **Drain the tomato can** before crushing.
- **Drain the mozzarella** before topping.
- **Pre-cook the bacon and mushrooms** before they touch the pizza.
- **Olive oil twice** — before the bake and after.
- **Nothing green goes in the oven.**
- **Knead at the speed where the dough gathers (5–6 on this mixer), not at the number a KitchenAid manual says.**
- **When the dough breaks, rest it — don't knead it, don't flour it.**
- **Zero sugar in the dough** (it burns at 500°C).
- **Watch the dough, not the clock** — especially in warm weather.
- The first pizza of any session is a calibration sacrifice. Eat it yourself.
- Dawood's dough will never look like Vito's on camera. It will still taste excellent. **That's the flour, not him.**
