# Yeast and temperature — research note (2026-09-07)

Trigger: the app said "whisk the yeast and honey into the water" with poolish water at ~5 °C in a warm kitchen. A poolish
made that way sat 10 hours without fermenting. Questions: does yeast go dormant below 10 °C, does cold water kill it,
and what water temperature should a poolish use?

## Conclusions

1. **Cold does not kill yeast.** Below about 10 °C baker's yeast slows to a crawl (the engine's cold-rate table gives
   4 °C ≈ 4 % of the 25 °C rate), in the fridge it idles, frozen it pauses; Saf-instant itself may be stored frozen.
   Heat kills: fermentation stops near 50 °C and cells die above ~55 °C. Never use water above 43 °C on yeast.
2. **Rehydrating dry yeast in cold water does the damage.** Instant and active dry yeast must come back to life at
   37–43 °C. Below 20 °C the cell wall recovers too slowly, up to half of the cell's soluble contents leak, part of the
   population is killed or crippled, and the leaked glutathione makes the dough slack and sticky. The manufacturer of
   the yeast Dawood uses prints the rule on the pack. Sugar (honey) in that first water adds osmotic stress.
3. **The failed poolish was the app's instruction.** "Whisk the yeast and honey into the water" with 5 °C water is
   exactly the forbidden case, in the most sensitive yeast type. It was not the poolish going "dormant" and it was not
   the cold as such; it was cold rehydration.
4. **What the app now says.** Suspend the yeast in 30 g of the poolish water at 38 °C, water only, 10 minutes. The rest
   of the poolish water keeps the designed cool temperatures (8 °C in a ≥ 30 °C kitchen, 12 °C at 27–29 °C, room water
   at 19–22 °C, 25 °C below 19 °C): cold main water is what holds the counter phase back in the heat (poolish-science
   R6, validated 8–10 °C), and hydrated yeast is only slowed by cold. Thermal check for a 330 g poolish with 31 °C
   flour: all water at 8 °C → 15 °C start; 30 g at 38 °C + 135 g at 8 °C → 18 °C start; the old whisk-in-cold method
   → 12 °C. The warm slurry does not accelerate the poolish; 10 minutes at 38 °C rehydrates cells, it does not grow
   them. Add the honey with the main water, not with the yeast. Fresh yeast skips all of this.
5. **Ice water in the final dough is still correct** when the arithmetic asks for it: the yeast there is already
   alive inside the poolish, and Lehmann's rule applies — once hydrated, yeast may meet ice. The emergency
   (no-poolish) dough and any extra final-dough yeast get the 38 °C suspension first.
6. **What to look for next time.** A properly made poolish in a 28–31 °C kitchen shows small bubbles within 45 minutes
   and is domed and bubbling all over by 1.5 hours. No bubbles at 2 hours means dead or damaged yeast, not a slow one:
   test the yeast (half a teaspoon in warm water with a pinch of sugar, foam within 15–30 minutes) before blaming the
   schedule.
