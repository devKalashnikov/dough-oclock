# Research notes — dimension: freezing-and-leftovers

Context: Dawood, Islamabad. CasaKoa gas oven (floor 380–400 °C, 60–90 s bake). Flour = weak maida (~9–10.5 % protein, no W rating; treat as W160–210) + 20 % fine chakki atta. Saf-Instant IDY. Established method = Vito poolish → 65–70 % hydration final dough, 3.5 % salt, ~5 % oil, 280 g balls, room-temp ball proof ~3–3.5 h at 28 °C. Fridge 3–6 °C (can drift to 7–8 °C). Sept kitchen 28–34 °C day / 24–27 °C night; winter 12–18 °C.

Research question: what to do with the dough balls that are NOT baked in the first sitting — fridge vs freezer thresholds, freezer → bake timelines by room temperature, what happens to a fully proofed ball that is refrigerated, par-baking, day-by-day storage behaviour.

Raw scrapes are in `research/raw/` (pmq-big-freeze.md, pm-46616.md, ka-freeze.md, vito-7day.md, avpn2008.txt, exa-*.txt).

## 2. Findings with numbers

### 2.1 FREEZING — what the experts say (verbatim where it matters)

**Tom Lehmann, PMQ "The Big Freeze" (S1):**
> "you'd be putting the dough into a static freezer at 0°F to -5°F ... This is referred to as 'slow freezing.' Slow freezing sets a very large ice crystal size, which is deleterious to yeast survival. For this reason, when dough is slow frozen, it has a frozen shelf life of only 10 to 15 days, during which time it will exhibit consistent performance after thawing (slacking out). For this reason, we recommend that dough, when slow frozen, be given a 10-day frozen shelf life; this will still give you three to five days to use the dough past its expiration date."

Lehmann's 7-point frozen-dough modifications (S1):
1. Use IDY.
2. "Prehydrate the IDY in a small quantity of 95°F water for 10 minutes before adding it to the dough."
3. "Since some of the yeast is damaged during the freezing process, it is recommended that the yeast level be increased by ¼ over what you would normally use in a fresh dough system. For example, if you normally use IDY at 0.375% ... increase the yeast level to 0.468% (round to 0.5%)."
4. Ascorbic acid 90–180 ppm (commercial; not relevant at home).
5. "Adjust the water temperature to give you a finished dough temperature in the 65°F-to-70°F range. This will probably mean using water at about 45°F."
6. "Limit the amount of fermentation that the dough receives prior to freezing by taking the dough directly from the mixer to the bench for scaling and balling. Immediately tray it and place it into the freezer until it is solidly frozen (about four to six hours). Note: I like to partially flatten the dough balls to reduce their cross-section."
7. "As soon as the dough balls are frozen, wipe them lightly with oil and bulk package them ... label with a date of production and a use-by date."

Lehmann thaw (S1): "placed onto lightly oiled sheet pans, or into dough boxes, and allowed to thaw overnight in the cooler for use on the following day." AIB improved method: thaw overnight in cooler → "allow them to stand at room temperature for one hour" → back into cooler "for use on the following day ... results in a crispier, more flavorful finished pizza crust."

**Lehmann on pizzamaking.com (S2), home context:**
> "As a general rule the only change to a dough formula that will be subjected to home freezing is to increase the yeast level by approximately 50%, the exact amount of increase will need to be determined through experimentation."

> "Assuming that the dough is slow/static frozen with the temperature range of +10 to -10F the dough will exhibit a frozen shelf life of 10 to 15-days during which it will produce acceptably consistent performance characteristics, with storage times beyond that performance becomes spotty and inconsistent at best. In order to achieve maximum frozen shelf life (12 to 21-weeks) the dough must be blast frozen ... In a home setting we also have to take into account the impact of freeze-thaw which is induced by the frost-free feature ... (as many as 24-cycles in a 24-hour period) is extremely deleterious to the viability of the yeast."

> "the more sugar you can add to a dough that is to be frozen the better ... both salt and sugars are solubles which depress the freezing point of water which helps to mitigate some of the ice crystal damage to the yeast cells ... all of those damaged yeast cells will be releasing the glutathione ... this glutathione will act on the protein to weaken the dough."

> "doughs made using bakers yeast are better suited to freezing than those made using a sourdough."

GoatGuy (S2, hobbyist experiments): "you will lose from 50% to 85% of the cells using commercial 'normal' freezers ... In my experiments, I found that 250% of a recipe's yeast load was needed to give viable dough 10 days after being non-blast frozen." (anecdotal, outlier)

**Pizzablab (S8)** — best-structured home guidance:

| | Frozen Before Fermentation | Frozen After Fermentation |
|---|---|---|
| Best For | Commercial use; when dough performance and consistency are the top priorities. | Home use; leftover dough balls; when dough performance is not a major concern. |
| Control Level | High (full control over fermentation after thawing). | Minimal-to-none (once thawed, must be used immediately). |
| Effect of Ice-Crystal Damage | Low-moderate weakening; gluten still retains gas well; minimal yeast damage. | Significant damage; higher gas-cell collapse, loss of elasticity, substantial yeast death. |
| Effect on Dough Structure | Minimal damage. | Significant; glutathione leakage and gluten disruption → weaker, fragile, tear-prone dough. |
| Baking Performance | Good; minimal compromise in oven spring/crumb. | Reduced; limited oven spring, noticeably denser crumb. |
| Sensitivity to Freeze–Thaw Cycles | Lower. | Higher; quality drops rapidly with storage time and temperature swings. |
| Usability Window (After Thawing) | Long (dough needs time to ferment). | Short (already fermented, loses strength quickly). |

Numbers (S8): "up to 50% of the yeast can be damaged"; "use 1.5 to 2 times more yeast ... I suggest starting with a 1.5x increase"; "a 300g dough ball might take nearly a full day to freeze completely in a home freezer, whereas an industrial blast freezer can achieve the same result in just one hour"; "the dough performs at its peak for up to two weeks after freezing ... While frozen dough remains usable for three months or more, it will not produce the same level of quality"; "keep your final dough temperature ... no higher than 20°C (68°F)"; "Flatten each ball into a 'puck' or disc shape about 3 cm (1.5 inches) thick"; "Thaw the dough in the fridge or at room temperature until it reaches an internal temperature of about 15°C (59°F) ... then proceed with your normal fermentation process"; "If freezing after fermentation ... Once thawed, the dough must be used immediately; it generally cannot be held in the fridge for another day"; "fresh yeast is better suited for dough intended for the freezer"; "Freezing leftover dough balls from a party is a common practice. While this dough will not perform nearly as well as fresh dough, for most home bakers, the results are 'good enough'."

**King Arthur (S7)** — freezes AFTER the first rise (opposite of Lehmann/Pizzablab):
> "Once the dough has gone through its first rise, divide, wrap, and freeze it."
> "if your recipe doesn't already include fat, replace 2 to 3 tablespoons of the recipe's water with olive or vegetable oil. Doing this will help reduce the formation of ice crystals."
> "You may also choose to increase the recipe's yeast by 50%, especially if you want to keep the dough frozen for longer than a couple of weeks."
> "Give the dough enough time to double in size before freezing. While it's fine to include some overnight cold fermentation ... don't try freezing a lump of leftover dough that's been hidden in the fridge for days."
> Freeze uncovered on a sheet in the coldest part, ~0°F, "After about an hour ... it's ready to wrap." "Wrap the dough 'skin tight' in plastic, then place in a plastic bag ... best to store pizza dough in a freezer that's not self-defrosting."
> "We suggest freezing pizza dough no longer than a month."
> "dead yeast releases a substance called glutathione, which acts as a natural dough relaxer. So frozen and thawed pizza dough is actually easier to shape than fresh dough."
> Thaw: "leave yourself 10 to 12 hours ... Let them thaw in the refrigerator for six to 10 hours, until they're soft all the way through. Flattened rounds will thaw a bit faster than balls."
> Final rise: "In a warm kitchen, making thin-crust pizza and using healthy dough, plan on around 90 minutes ... a thick-crust pizza rising in a coolish kitchen could take up to 5 hours."
> Comment quoting Gemignani, The Pizza Bible p.49: frozen balls "into 80 degree water for 15 minutes ... then sit at room temp for 1 and a half to 2 hours until thawed the whole way through."

**Serious Eats / Kenji (S10):** "You can freeze a homemade dough at essentially any point in the process and it'll work just fine ... it makes sense to freeze dough as far along the process as possible." He freezes after bulk (incl. cold ferment), balled, on parchment, then bags; to use, "allow them to proof as normal ... tacking on an extra couple hours to allow them to defrost before they really start to rise."

**Ooni (S12, S13):** ball, "Lightly oil the sides of several airtight, lidded containers", "Store your dough in the freezer for up to 3 months." Defrost: "Transfer your dough to the refrigerator the day before ... Remove dough from the refrigerator at least 6 hours before you want to cook"; water bath ~30 min. Product insert (UK): "Optimal: place the dough in the fridge for 12-36 hours for a cold prove. Short on time: Defrost at room temperature (18-22° C) for a minimum of 5-6 hours. In a hurry: ... over warm water (not boiling!) for 2-3 hours." Then "gently shape into smooth balls ... prove at room temperature (18-22° C) for 2-4 hours before cooking. Top tip: the warmer the room the faster the prove". "Once defrosted use dough within 3 days." "do not refreeze after thawing." US insert: fridge 12–24 h, or RT 68–72 °F at least 5 h; space 1" apart with damp towel.

**Baking Steel (S15):** "freeze it right after balling, store for up to 45 days, then thaw in the fridge for 24 hours and let it rest at room temp for 3-4 hours"; "Always freeze your dough after the bulk fermentation"; "I've pushed it to 60 days and it's still okay, but 45 is the sweet spot"; optional "about 1.5x" yeast; "Can I refreeze? I wouldn't recommend it."

**ATK (S18):** refrigerate 24 h → ball → "freeze until firm, about 3 hours" → bag "for up to 2 weeks"; thaw "in refrigerator for 12 to 24 hours."

**Iowa State Extension (S17):** freeze between first and second proof; "Increasing the yeast by ¼ to ½ teaspoon per 3 cups (360 grams) of flour" (≈ +0.8–1.6 g IDY per 360 g = +0.2–0.45 % of flour); "flash freeze ... 1-2 hours"; "Dough may be frozen up to 1 month"; thaw refrigerator "8-16 hours", countertop "4-9 hours", warm location "2-4 hours"; "Do not over proof."

### 2.2 FREEZING — peer-reviewed numbers

- Isache 2026 (S25): dough frozen to core −5/−10/−15 °C then stored −18 °C up to 56 days. "Samples frozen to −15 °C consistently exhibited superior fermentative capacity"; "A progressive decline in yeast activity and product quality was observed with increasing storage time, reaching reductions of approximately 30% after 56 days."
- Autio & Sinda 1992 (S26): "The viability decreased slightly during two weeks of storage at -18 C. Dead yeast cells did not affect the rheological properties" (but G′ decreased — dough softened).
- Meziani et al. 2012 (S27): freezing rate controls ice crystal size and location; liquid-nitrogen freezing caused "70% decrease on yeast population" (osmotic shock — too fast is also bad); slow freezing at −20/−30 °C affected gluten integrity; dough volume loss "25%±2".
- Literature summary via search (S. cerevisiae metabolism paper, academia.edu 3388532; abstract not directly opened): viability reduced up to 47 % after 28 days (≈53–55 % survival); gassing power −30 to −35 % vs fresh; stable −18 °C storage preserved viability for 112 days, temperature fluctuations accelerate loss. Treat as medium confidence.
- Ribotta 2003 (S28): freezing/frozen storage of dough made with fresh yeast hurt baking quality more than adding pre-frozen yeast; leachates increase gluten solubility, reduce loaf volume.
- Wolt & D'Appolonia 1984 via S29: dead cells — compressed fresh yeast 4.9 %, ADY 13.0 %, IDY 18.6 % before freezing; fresh yeast "slightly better proof-time stability" over 20 weeks. BUT El-Hady 1996 via S29: after 12 weeks frozen, gas production fell 4 % for fresh vs 1.8 % for IDY — i.e., the literature is contradictory on fresh vs IDY; differences are small either way.
- Luhila 2024 (S30): 8-week frozen storage; ice-binding proteins improved yeast survival by ~7.8 % (shows baseline loss is modest per week at stable −18 °C).

### 2.3 REFRIGERATED HOLD — how many days

**Lehmann Dough Management Procedure (S3), high-protein US flour, ~0.375 % IDY, 45 °F/7 °C cooler:**
> "13. The dough will be ready to use after 16 hours in the cooler. 14. To use the dough, remove about a 3-hour supply ... allow it to temper AT room temperature for 60 to 90-minutes ... 15. The dough will remain good to use for up to 3 hours after you first begin using it. 16. Any dough remaining in the cooler will keep for up to 3 days."
Elsewhere (PMQ Think Tank via search, S32 context): "Don't hold the dough for more than about 36 hours as the dough will most likely become over fermented beyond this point" — this was for a specific higher-yeast shop dough. Cooler-temperature targets (S32): "For 24-36 hour storage: 55°F is a good temperature to aim for"; "For 48+ hour storage: target a temperature of 50°F before down-stacking".
Lehmann dough-box column (S31): "the dough balls remaining in the boxes at room temperature will be good for a period of two and a half to three hours" (lidded); balls "spaced about 2" to 3" apart"; "oil the top of each dough ball".

**AVPN Disciplinare 2008 (S24), room-temp only, no fridge in the standard:**
> "First stage of dough rise 2 hours ... Form small balls of approximately 180-250 grams ... Second stage of dough rise ... 4-6 hours. Recommended levitation temperature Room temperature : 25°c. Conservation temperature As above for 6 hours following second stage of levitation." and "By controlling storage temperature (keeping at room temperature), these dough balls can then be used at any time for the following 6 hours."
→ AVPN's usable window for a ball at 25 °C is hours 4–12 after balling. No refrigeration guidance (the 2016+ versions allow controlled-temperature environments but were not readable here).

**Serious Eats 10-day test (S11, Kenji, high-protein bread flour, skillet/broiler):** each day a 6 oz piece balled, proofed 2 h at 70 °F, baked. Day-1 pie "bakes to a relatively uniform shade of brown and has a pretty tight, small-holed structure". Conclusion: "The Ideal Cold Fermentation Period: Three to Five Days". Beyond: "as the alcohol content and acidity of the dough rises, eventually it gets so high that the yeast simply cease to operate ... The dough barely rises, and the structure of the bread suffers."

**Jayarr fridge table (S19):**

| Duration | Flavor | Texture | Handling | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 hours | Noticeable improvement over same-day | Good extensibility | Slightly softer than fresh | The minimum for quality |
| 48 hours | Robust complexity, subtle sharpness | Very extensible | Softer, more delicate | Peak value for most bakers |
| 72 hours | Peak complexity, 50+ flavor compounds | Extremely extensible | Fragile, needs careful handling | Excellent if comfortable |
| 4-5 days | Good but diminishing returns | Structural degradation begins | Very fragile, wet | Possible with very low yeast and strong flour |
| 7 days | Intense, complex (Iacopelli: "amazing") | Sponge-like, very large holes | Extremely fragile | Exceptional but demanding |

Jayarr yeast-by-duration table (S19): Same-day 4–8 h RT: IDY 0.4–1 %; 24 h cold: 0.25–0.5 %; 48 h cold: 0.2–0.3 %; 72 h cold: 0.1–0.2 %.
Jayarr on flour: "Weak flour (10-11% protein, low W value) will lose its structure faster ... Gemignani explicitly insists on 12.5-14% protein flour for long cold ferments"; "Masi recommends 24-48 hour cold fermentation for strong flours (W 250+) and only 8-12 hours for weaker flours. Using a weak flour (W 160-200) with a 72-hour cold ferment risks complete structural collapse."

**Pizzalogic fridge (S20):** Day 1 "usable but still young ... mild"; Days 2–3 sweet spot; "Days 4–5 (96–120 hours): ... A strong bread flour or a high-W 00 flour (W280+) can handle this, but all-purpose flour usually can't - the gluten network starts falling apart, and the dough turns slack and sticky." Salt "2–3% ... inhibits the protease enzymes"; fridge "38°F–42°F (3°C–5°C)"; "For longer storage, freeze it after the initial rise ... ideally within the first day or two."

**Baking Steel fridge (S16):** 12–24 h "usable but hasn't developed much flavor"; 24–48 h "developing real flavor ... gluten relaxing"; 48–72 h "The sweet spot"; 72–96 h "Still good, but you're pushing it. The dough may start to get a little slack or develop a stronger fermented smell"; beyond 5 days "likely over-fermented ... flat, dense crust". "If you won't use it within 3–5 days, freeze it instead. Frozen dough keeps for up to 45 days."

**pizzaeveryfriday (S22):** "One week: consistently successful"; "Nine days: successful Margherita"; "Two weeks: ... 'gummy and dense' ... smelled very strongly of alcohol"; smell is the primary indicator. (Flour/yeast unspecified; likely bread flour + low yeast.)

**Vito Iacopelli 8 h / 48 h / 7 d (S23, transcript):** same recipe, three ages. 48 h "a little bit more soft compared to the other one"; 7-day "is a little bit more wet because a lot of humidity you're gonna use a little bit more flour ... more hydration ... more air"; "this is very fragile because of course the air inside is much more"; verdict "if I have to judge ... the one week old that's amazing". Baked at ~2 h after removal from fridge. NOTE: Vito uses strong Italian flour; on maida the 7-day dough would collapse (see S19 Masi/Gemignani warnings).

**pizzamaking 70393 (S4) — practical outcome for over-risen balls:** Caputo 00, 65 %, 0.5 % IDY at 38 °F: "already nearly doubled in size" at 24 h. Advice from an experienced member: "I would reball now, put into freezer, then let them thaw/rise again in fridge beginning tomorrow morning. Then a RT rest about 3 hours before bake ... Freezing only works well for me when doughballs are stored in plastic baggies." OP outcome: "I re-balled the dough balls and put them back in the fridge overnight. Pulled them the next day about 3 hours before stretching. The dough was quite possibly my favorite so far ... It skinned effortlessly and had wonderful pockets of crunchy airy spaces". Reply: "I have had doughs leftover from parties that I reballed then froze and used months later ... Even dough that is sitting in your fridge for over a week can have life after the reball." For very long cold ferments this member uses "probably closer to .1%" IDY.

**TXCraig1 (S5):** "10-15 minutes rest may not be enough after a reball. The dough may need several hours to relax to the point where it can be properly opened up into a skin." Alternative to re-balling: "less time in balls ... (for example 2 days bulk CF and 1 day ball rather than 3 days in balls)."

**Context handoff (previous session):** balls hold "another 18–24 hours" in the fridge without overproofing after balling; fridge >7–8 °C → overproofing risk.

### 2.4 Ball that was fully room-proofed, then refrigerated a day, then used (Q3)

- Lehmann (S3 reply, frozen-dough context): "crusts made from frozen dough that had been over fermented and re-rounded then allowed to rest again until the dough balls could be easily opened into skins always exhibited super flavor characteristics." → over-proofed + re-rounded + rested = good flavor; this is the origin of his 1 h RT → 24 h cold method.
- Lehmann (S31): boxed balls at RT are good 2.5–3 h; DMP: "good to use for up to 3 hours after you first begin using it". So a ball proofed 3–3.5 h at 28 °C is at the END of Lehmann's usable window when the first sitting ends.
- S4 (real outcome): balls that doubled early → re-balled → fridge overnight → 3 h RT → excellent.
- Iacopelli via S19: "gently reshape the dough (releasing excess gas), let it rest for 1 hour, and it recovers."
- Gozney (S33): knock-back = fingertip test 2 s; if dent stays, press out gas with palm/fist, "Reball over-proofing dough balls" (no rest time given).
- TXCraig1 (S5): after re-ball, "several hours" not 10–15 min.
- Pizzablab (S8) on freezing after fermentation: gluten already stretched and chemically weakened → structure damage worse → must use immediately after thaw, no fridge hold afterwards.
- Serious Eats (S11): over-aged dough "barely rises" at final proof — i.e., yeast exhaustion, not just gluten.
Synthesis: a fully proofed 280 g maida ball put in the fridge is USABLE next day if (a) re-balled (degassed) before refrigerating, (b) fridge ≤ 5 °C, (c) pulled 2–3 h before use at 25–28 °C (longer if cooler), (d) used within ~24 h; expect softer, more extensible, slightly flatter cornicione but better flavor. Beyond 24 h on weak maida, expect slack/sticky and reduced spring → freeze instead.

### 2.5 Par-baking at Neapolitan temperature (Q4)

- Ooni (S14): "We recommend about 30 seconds, which gives it enough time to cook while still being pale in color." Cool flipped on a rack, "Wrap the pizzas tightly in plastic wrap and freeze ... They can last for months in the freezer." "While this isn't an ideal way to cook Neapolitan style, it will result in some tasty pies, especially pan pizzas."
- pizzamaking 60632 (S6), WFO 700–900 °F, Neapolitan dough:
  - Tom Lehmann: "Try par-baking with 1/2 of the sauce, then remove from oven and place on screens in a wire tree rack to cool. Does not need refrigeration. At the event all you need to do is to apply the remainder of the sauce and dress to the order ... Reheating a par-baked pizza is OK at best."
  - Member: "Prebaking with some sauce is necessary to prevent the crust to 'blow up'; it will get too airy. Using all sauce for the prebake results in too dry pizza after the final bake. I can confirm that 50/50 will work best."
  - Member: par-baking without sauce → "big air bubbles"; use a dough docker; cooled crusts can be stacked and bagged; "If your final bake is some days out, the crusts can be refrigerated and or frozen".
  - WFO operator "AM": "I cook them to at least 90% done. You can pre-cook them even 3-4 hours prior. Just do not refrigerate the pies. Leave them at room temp. When ready to serve pop them back in the oven for about 30-40 seconds ... As long as you are using fior di latte / fresh mozzarella it works out well. Does not work well with the dry plastic wrapped mozz."
- King Arthur reader (S7 comments): shells baked "at 500 degrees on a pizza stone for around 6 minutes", cooled, frozen in bags; thawed briefly, topped, baked.
Synthesis: viable at 450 °C only as a 30–45 s "set" bake with half the sauce (or docked), cooled on a rack, held at room temp for up to a few hours or frozen wrapped; final bake ~40–60 s. Result is crisper and less Neapolitan; experts rate it "OK at best". For a leftover-dough problem it is inferior to re-balling + fridge/freezer, but it IS the best way to turn extra balls into something that keeps for weeks without thaw-timing risk.

### 2.6 Storing balled dough in a home fridge (Q5) and day-by-day changes

- Lehmann (S31): oil the TOP of each ball; "spaced about 2" to 3" apart to allow for expansion" (softer/higher-hydration doughs need more); lidded.
- Ooni (S12): individual "airtight, lidded containers", sides lightly oiled. Ooni insert (S13): "Space out 1" apart in a large tray with a damp towel or in proving containers with a lid."
- King Arthur (S7): "lightly flour or oil the surface of the dough"; for freezing, "skin tight" plastic + bag.
- pizzaeveryfriday (S22): 1-quart deli containers (slim, but hard to extract), wider 1-quart containers (preferred, dough spreads horizontally, easier removal), Ooni Stack glass for ≤250 g; "lids are essential to prevent drying".
- Pizzablab (S8): for freezing, flatten to 3 cm pucks, oil dough or container.
- Context handoff: oiled tray, 5 cm+ spacing, cling wrap oiled on the underside, no holes.
- Day-by-day (fridge 3–5 °C, ball entered fresh): Day 1 (12–24 h): "clean, mild flavor" (S20), "usable but hasn't developed much flavor yet" (S16), day-1 bake "uniform brown ... tight, small-holed" (S11). Day 2–3 (48–72 h): "sweet spot" (S16, S20), "very extensible, easy stretch, softer, more fragile" (S19). Day 4–5: "pushing it ... slack ... stronger fermented smell" (S16); "all-purpose flour usually can't" (S20); "soupy and difficult to shape" (S19). Day 7: "fragile, very wet, needs extra flour" (Iacopelli via S19/S23). Day 9: still OK for one tester (S22). Day 14: "gummy and dense", strong alcohol smell (S22).
- Over-fermentation signs (S16, S20, S19): smells like alcohol/nail-polish remover or vinegar; liquid pooling; grey/dark spots; permanent finger dent, no spring-back; collapsed or sunken; tears immediately. Sour-milk/rancid smell or fuzzy mould = discard.
- Ball behaviour: balls flatten/spread over 2–3 days in the fridge (S5) — "A flat dough ball doesn't, in and of itself, say anything about the need for a reball" (TXCraig1). Large surface bubbles after cold ferment are normal (context handoff).

## 3. Contradictions between sources and how to resolve them

1. **Freeze BEFORE fermentation (Lehmann S1, Pizzablab S8) vs AFTER bulk/first rise (King Arthur S7, Serious Eats S10, Baking Steel S15, ATK S18, Iowa State S17).**
   Resolution: both camps agree on the mechanism; they differ on priority. Before-ferment preserves gas retention and gives a full, controllable fermentation after thaw (commercial priority). After-ferment locks in flavour and needs only warming after thaw (home convenience). For Dawood the choice is forced: leftover balls have ALREADY fermented (poolish + 3–3.5 h proof). So the engine must treat leftover freezing as "frozen after fermentation": short post-thaw window, no fridge hold after thaw, weaker structure, use within 14 days for predictability. If the user plans ahead to freeze a portion, the engine can offer the "freeze right after balling, before the room proof" option, which is closer to the Lehmann/Pizzablab ideal.

2. **Yeast increase: +25 % (Lehmann commercial, S1) vs +50 % (Lehmann home S2; King Arthur S7) vs 1.5–2× (Pizzablab S8) vs 2.5× (GoatGuy S2) vs none needed (Baking Steel says "just dormant", Ooni, Kenji).**
   Resolution: for dough frozen BEFORE fermentation, +50 % IDY (1.5×) is the modal home recommendation and matches "up to 50 % yeast damage". For dough frozen AFTER a full proof (Dawood's leftovers) the yeast has already done its work; no yeast bump is applied (you cannot bump yeast in only the leftover fraction anyway). GoatGuy's 2.5× is an outlier for 10-day storage in a bad freezer; ignore.

3. **Freezer life: 10–15 days (Lehmann static freezer, S1/S2) vs 2 weeks peak / 3 months usable (Pizzablab S8, ATK 2 weeks S18) vs 1 month (King Arthur S7, Iowa S17) vs 45–60 days (Baking Steel S15) vs 2 months (Jayarr S19) vs 3 months (Ooni S12, Masi via S19).**
   Resolution: the peer-reviewed data show ~slight loss at 2 weeks (S26), ~45 % viability loss by 28 days (search summary), ~30 % activity loss by 56 days at stable −18 °C (S25), worse with fluctuation (S2, S27). Everyone agrees quality declines monotonically. Engine: "best" ≤ 14 days; "acceptable" ≤ 30 days; refuse/flag > 30 days (Pakistani home freezers with load-shedding freeze–thaw cycles are closer to Lehmann's worst case than to Ooni's 3 months).

4. **Fresh yeast better for freezing (Pizzablab S8; Wolt & D'Appolonia via S29) vs IDY recommended (Lehmann S1; El-Hady via S29 found IDY lost less).**
   Resolution: differences are a few percent; Lehmann's IDY choice is about consistency. Keep Saf-Instant; do not tell the user to hunt khameer for freezing.

5. **Fridge hold: Lehmann "up to 3 days" (S3) / "36 hours" for a hotter shop dough; Serious Eats 3–5 days ideal (S11); Jayarr/Baking Steel 48–72 h sweet spot; pizzaeveryfriday 7–9 days OK; Vito 7 days "amazing" (S23) — all on strong flour (12–14 % protein or W260+).**
   Resolution for weak maida (~W160–210 equivalent): Masi 8–12 h cold for weak flour (S19), Pizzablab table 8–16 h for W160–210 (S9), Pizzalogic "AP can't do 4–5 days" (S20), Jayarr "W160–200 with 72 h risks complete structural collapse". Mitigants in Dawood's formula: 3.5 % salt (protease inhibition, S20), fridge at 3–5 °C, 20 % atta (bran enzymes — slightly worse). Engine: for already-proofed maida leftovers, fridge ≤ 24 h (hard stop 36 h); for freshly balled cold maida dough, ≤ 48 h (hard stop 72 h). Beyond → freezer or par-bake.

6. **Rest after re-ball: 1 h (Iacopelli via S19) vs "several hours" (TXCraig1 S5) vs ~3 h RT after overnight fridge (S4) vs 10–15 min (novice in S5, judged insufficient).**
   Resolution: temperature-dependent. At 28 °C, 1.5–2 h after a re-ball is enough; at 15 °C, 3–4 h. If re-balled and refrigerated overnight, temper 2–3 h at 25–28 °C (S4 used 3 h at a US room temp ~22 °C).

7. **Thaw at room temperature: 2–4 h at 21–24 °C (S20), 3–5 h (S34, 400 g), 4–5 h (S21), 5–6 h at 18–22 °C (S13), 4–9 h (S17).**
   Resolution: the spread comes from ball mass (250–400 g), puck vs ball, and what "thawed" means (ice gone vs ready to stretch). For a 280 g ball flattened to a 3 cm puck: ~4.5 h at 20 °C to reach ~15 °C core; scale ≈ inversely with (T_room − 0 °C) — see engine table.

8. **Par-bake: Ooni 30 s pale, freeze for months (S14) vs Lehmann half-sauce, cool, no fridge, "OK at best" (S6) vs AM 90 % done, hold 3–4 h RT, 30–40 s finish (S6).**
   Resolution: these are three different products (blank shell / half-dressed shell / near-finished pizza). All agree it is not true Neapolitan. Engine offers it only as a fallback when the leftover would otherwise exceed freezer limits or the user has no freezer space.

9. **Water-bath thaw: unwrapped balls straight into 80 °F water (Gemignani, S7 comment) vs sealed bag only (King Arthur, S20, S21, S36).**
   Resolution: engine recommends sealed-bag lukewarm (24–27 °C) bath only as an emergency path; never unwrapped.

## 4. Recommendations for the deterministic engine

Definitions: `t_ball` = moment balls are formed; `t_bake1` = first bake; `t_bake2` = remainder bake; `T_room` = user-entered kitchen temperature (°C); `n_left` = pizzas − eaten in first sitting.

### 4.1 Decision: fridge vs freezer vs par-bake (weak maida dough, balls already proofed at t_bake1)

| Δ = t_bake2 − t_bake1 | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 3 h and T_room ≤ 28 °C | Leave covered at room temp | Lehmann: boxed balls good 2.5–3 h at RT (S31); AVPN: 6 h window at 25 °C (S24). At 30–34 °C cap this at 1.5–2 h. |
| 3 h < Δ ≤ 24 h | **Re-ball immediately after first sitting → oiled, sealed container → fridge (3–5 °C)** | S4 outcome; Lehmann re-round/rest flavour note (S3). Pull 2–3 h before t_bake2 at 25–28 °C (see 4.3). |
| 24 h < Δ ≤ 36 h | Fridge is still allowed but flag "reduced oven spring likely — re-ball again 2 h before baking" | Weak-flour limit (S9 W180 ≈ 12 h cold from mix; the balls already carried ~4 h of warm fermentation). Lehmann 36 h remark. |
| Δ > 36 h (or fridge known to run > 6 °C) | **Freezer** (re-ball → flatten to 3 cm puck → oil → skin-tight cling + bag → coldest shelf, not door) | S1, S7, S8. Frozen-after-fermentation: use within 14 days for predictable results; 30 days hard max. |
| Δ > 30 days or no freezer | Par-bake 30–45 s with half the sauce, cool on rack, bag; fridge ≤ 2 days or freeze | S6, S14. Finish 40–60 s at 400 °C floor. |

If the user tells the engine IN ADVANCE that `n_left` balls will be frozen (planning mode), the engine should instead instruct: freeze those balls right after balling, before the room proof (Lehmann S1 / Pizzablab S8 "before fermentation"), flattened to pucks, and note that a +50 % IDY bump is only applied if the user splits the final dough into two mixes (otherwise the same-day balls would over-proof). Default: no yeast bump; instead add +1 h to the post-thaw proof.

### 4.2 Freezer → bake timeline (280 g, frozen-after-proof leftover, flattened puck)

Preferred path (S1, S7, S12, S15, S18): move to fridge 12–24 h before t_bake2 (engine schedules "move to fridge" at t_bake2 − 16 h, window 12–24 h), then room-temperature temper:

| T_room | RT temper after fridge thaw (frozen-after-proof leftover) | RT temper after fridge thaw (frozen-before-proof, needs full proof) |
|---|---|---|
| 12–15 °C | 4 h | 6–7 h |
| 16–20 °C | 3 h | 4.5–5 h |
| 21–24 °C | 2.5 h | 3.5–4 h |
| 25–28 °C | 2 h | 3–3.5 h (= the established 28 °C ball proof) |
| 29–34 °C | 1.5 h | 2–2.5 h |
Sources: Ooni "at least 6 h" at 18–22 °C after fridge thaw for a full prove (S12/S13); Baking Steel 3–4 h (S15); KA ~90 min in a warm kitchen for thin crust (S7); jayarr/Pizzalogic 1–2 h temper after fridge thaw (S19, S20); Lehmann 60–90 min temper for cold balls (S3). Numbers above interpolate those with Q10-style scaling (fermentation rate roughly doubles per 8–10 °C).

Emergency counter thaw from frozen (no fridge step), 280 g puck, sealed in bag, then proceed to temper above: 
| T_room | Time to thaw to ~15 °C core |
|---|---|
| 15 °C | 5.5–6 h |
| 20 °C | 4.5–5 h (S13: 5–6 h at 18–22 °C; S17: 4–9 h; S21: 4–5 h) |
| 25 °C | 3.5 h |
| 30 °C | 2.5–3 h |
| 34 °C | 2–2.5 h |
Scale ≈ 90 h·°C ÷ T_room for a 3 cm puck (calibrated to 4.5 h at 20 °C); a round un-flattened ball adds ~40 %. Sealed lukewarm water bath (≤ 27 °C): 45–60 min + 30–45 min temper (S20) — emergency only. Never refreeze (S13, S15).

### 4.3 Fridge → bake timeline for a re-balled leftover (already proofed once)

Pull from fridge at t_bake2 − X where X by T_room: 12–15 °C: 3.5–4 h; 16–20 °C: 3 h; 21–24 °C: 2.5 h; 25–28 °C: 2 h; 29–34 °C: 1.5 h. Poke test at X − 0.5 h. If the ball has spread flat and is very slack at pull time, re-ball gently and give 1.5–2 h at ≥ 25 °C (S5, S19).

### 4.4 Numeric limits to hard-code

- Freezer quality windows: best ≤ 14 d (S1, S8, S18); acceptable ≤ 30 d (S7, S17); warn ≥ 31 d; refuse ≥ 90 d.
- Time to freeze solid at home: 4–6 h (S1) to ~24 h for a 300 g ball (S8) → engine tells user "leave uncovered on a tray 2–3 h, then wrap" (S7: ~1 h at 0 °F; S17: 1–2 h) and to keep pucks ≤ 3 cm thick.
- Fridge hold limits (maida): leftover proofed balls 24 h (hard 36 h); fresh cold-balled dough 48 h (hard 72 h). For 00/strong flour the same limits would be 48–72 h and 72–120 h (S3, S11, S16, S19).
- After thaw: use within the same day; do not hold thawed leftover balls in the fridge another day (S8). (Ooni's "within 3 days" applies to their un-proofed frozen product, S13.)
- Room-temp life of a proofed ball: 2.5–3 h at ~22 °C (S31); 6 h at 25 °C per AVPN (S24) — engine uses 3 h at 25 °C, 2 h at 30 °C, 1.5 h at 34 °C.
- Storage geometry: 5–7.5 cm between balls on a tray (S31 2–3"), or one ball per ≥ 1-quart (≈1 L) lidded container (S22); oil the tops; no pinholes in wrap.
- Yeast bump when freezing un-proofed dough in a separate mix: ×1.5 IDY (S2, S7, S8, S15); prehydrate IDY 10 min in 35 °C water (S1); final dough temperature ≤ 20 °C (S8) / 18–21 °C (S1).

### 4.5 Quality expectations to print
- Refrigerated leftover (≤ 24 h): better flavour than day-of, softer/more extensible, slightly lower cornicione. 
- Frozen leftover: "good enough" (S8), easier to stretch (glutathione, S7), flatter cornicione, denser crumb, less leoparding; expect 10–30 % less rise.
- Par-baked: crisper, less Neapolitan; "OK at best" (Lehmann, S6).
