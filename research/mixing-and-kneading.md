# Mixing and kneading in a home stand mixer — research note (2026-09-07)

Trigger: a batch mixed by the app's earlier method ("flours + poolish + 80 % of the water, speed 1 for 3–4 min, rest,
salt, remaining water, knead 8–10 min on speed 1–2, never above speed 2") stayed liquid, never formed a ball and
never pulled from the bowl walls. Dawood's observation: everyone he asks kneads at high speed in the middle so the
dough "forms the structure". Question 2: is ice better than cold water?

## Conclusions

1. **Speed 2 is the kneading speed on a KitchenAid-class mixer; speed 1 only stirs.** The old text "speed 1–2" read as
   speed 1, which on a planetary mixer needs 17–25 min to build any structure. Everyone Dawood sees speeding up
   is on a spiral mixer (~200 rpm on its second speed) or a commercial planetary; a KitchenAid's speed 1 already
   turns faster than a spiral's second speed, and its gearbox, not the dough, is why the maker caps it at speed 2.
2. **The liquid dough was mostly water added too early.** Poolish (100 % hydration, 25 % of the flour) plus 80 % of
   the remaining water puts the first mix at ~57 % total hydration, above the 40–50 % that weak, slow-absorbing
   flour needs to link gluten. Diluted strands slide past each other and the hook spins in vain (Wordloaf) — the
   exact symptom. New first mix: poolish + **half** the final water ≈ 45–47 % total, speed 1 to a rough tight ball,
   rest 15–20 min, speed 2 until it clings to the hook, then the rest of the water in 3–4 absorbed additions.
3. **Salt**: dissolved in the held-back water and added with it. Slightly delayed (the validated practice, and what
   Vito does), dissolved so it distributes evenly (PizzaBlab). Oil only after all the water is in.
4. **Stop at smooth and satiny.** For overnight or cold-fermented dough, intensive mixing is wrong for weak flour;
   fermentation finishes the gluten. A full windowpane is not the goal.
5. **Ice.** The app already back-calculates water temperature from a desired dough temperature; with a 30 °C kitchen
   and 30 °C flour the answer is near 0 °C, i.e. ice water (ice melted in, removed before weighing). Below 0 °C the
   maths cannot be met with liquid water: replace 20 % of the water with crushed ice by weight in the first mix and
   chill the flour/bowl, accepting a slightly longer first mix. Friction factor raised to **17 °C** for a ~15-min
   speed-2 knead (King Arthur 12–13 °C at 7 min, PizzaBlab 8–16 °C range, longer/heavier → higher). Measure the
   dough once after mixing and correct the water by the difference next time.
6. **If it still fails**: a hook that spins without catching is a bowl too big for the batch (hold water back,
   scrape, 5 more min, or finish by hand with slap-and-folds); strandy or tearing dough gets a 15-min covered rest
   then 3 min on speed 2; dough above 27 °C gets stretch-and-folds instead of more mixer time. Never add flour.
