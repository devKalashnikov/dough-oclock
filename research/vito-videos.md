### 2.1 V1 — NEXT LEVEL PIZZA DOUGH, DOUBLE FERMENTED + POOLISH (2021-06-11)

Poolish (spoken):

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Water | 300 ml (no temperature stated; Napo write-up says cold) |
| Honey | 5 g |
| Fresh yeast | 6 g — "if you're using dry yeast simply just put five grams" |
| 00 flour | 300 g |

- "let it rest one hour at room temperature and then we are gonna put it in the fridge for 16 to 24 hours … after six hours the fermentation process will start" (i.e. he expects the real activity to happen inside the fridge).
- After 16 h the poolish "is a little bit flat … this is all normal".
- "you can use it directly from the fridge … but the best is to wait about 30 minutes that the poolish gets back to temperature".

Final dough "for one kilo of flour":

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| All the poolish | (300 + 300 + 5 + 5) |
| Water | 400 ml, added to the poolish first, poolish dissolved |
| Sea salt | **25 g spoken** ("25 grams of sea salt"); **30 g in the Napo / cooked.wiki write-ups** |
| 00 flour | 700 g — "if you don't have 00 … just use any flour … the pizza will come out perfect anyway" |
| Olive oil | 10 g, added at the end of kneading; "the olive oil will help the cooking process … it will make your gluten a little bit more strong" |

- Totals: flour 1000 g, water 700 g → **70% hydration** ("this is 70 percent hydration"); salt 2.5–3.0%; IDY 5 g = **0.5% of total flour**; poolish flour = 30% of total flour.
- Order: salt dissolved in poolish + water first, then flour. Knead by hand 10–15 min. Add oil. Form ball, "close the dough for 15 to 20 minutes", then a few stretch/folds to smooth, then oiled sealed container ("no air"), **straight into the fridge for 16–24 h**.
- Next morning: "you have to keep the dough out from the fridge for about 30 minutes to one hour just to bring it at room temperature" before balling (balling cold "damages the gluten"). Balls "around 250 grams". Seam down in a closed box.
- Ball proof: "leave it at room temperature for about two hours and then the dough will be ready but **if it's hot it may take also only one hour** because remember we already give plenty of hours of fermentation … two hours at room temperature is best".
- Home oven: "warm up the oven at the max temperature with the stone inside for about one hour". Pizza 12 inch.
- Filmed in Los Angeles in June (kitchen probably ~24–26 °C; not stated).

### 2.2 V2 — 100% Poolish in Electric Oven (2023-10-21)

Poolish: **1 L cold water** ("make sure it needs to be cold the water"), **5 g dry yeast**, **5 g honey** (forgot then added; "if you don't have honey use organic sugar or brown sugar"), **1 kg flour**. Mix ~2 min. Plastic wrap with "a couple of holes not too much". **1 h room temp, 24 h fridge.**

Final dough (no water): poolish + **450 g 00 flour + 30 g salt + 5 g extra dry yeast**. Knead 5–10 min. **30 g olive oil** ("because we are cooking in an electric oven"). Rest 15–20 min ("20 minutes is best"), stretch/fold to smooth, oiled container, **fridge 24 h** (actually "16 … 20 to 24 hours").
- Totals: flour 1450 g, water 1000 g → **69% hydration**; salt 2.1%; IDY 10 g = **0.69%**; poolish flour = 69% of total flour ("100% poolish" in his terminology = no additional water).
- Next day: deflate the bulk, ball with oiled hands ("we don't want to add any flour anymore because otherwise you're going to change the hydration"), **280 g balls**, "four fingers apart", closed box, **room temp 1–2 h**; shown ready at 2 h ("double up the sides"). "Total 48 hours plus … about 50 hours of fermentation."
- "The secret of making the best pizza is making the pizza at the right point of fermentation which is in between from not grow to overgrow."
- Electric oven 250 °C.

### 2.3 V3 — In Depth Understanding Poolish % (2023)

- Poolish "you have to prepare 16 to 24 hours before": 300 ml water, 300 g flour, 5 g yeast, 5 g honey; 1 h room temp, fridge 16–24 h.
- **His flour rule: "every 100 millilitres of water goes 145 grams of flour"** (= 69% hydration).
- 100% poolish dough: 300 ml water in poolish × 1.45 = 435 g flour total − 300 g already in poolish = **135 g flour + 15 g salt** (salt 3.4% of flour). Mixed 1 min in bowl, 5–10 min on counter, olive oil on top, rest ~10 min.
- 20% poolish dough (for the same 300 ml total water): **100 g poolish (50 water + 50 flour) + 200 ml water + 15 g salt + 385 g flour**. Sticker than the 100% dough; ~10 min kneading.
- **Core statements:** "the more poolish we are going to use the fastest will grow your dough, it doesn't change the taste, it doesn't change anything, it's just the timing"; "**when it's a really hot day use less poolish, when it's a cold day use 100% poolish**"; "the more it's hot the less poolish you put, the more it's cold the more poolish you put".
- Timing observed: "100% of poolish versus 20% … this is gonna grow in one hour and this is gonna take about three to four hours to reach the same size". After 1 h the 100% had doubled; balled both; after 30 more minutes the 100% ball was ready ("double up the side"), the 20% "needs about maybe two hours or so, it's hot". At ~3 h total, "**we have about 75 Fahrenheit** [24 °C] and this is how the dough looks like", 20% ready.
- Result: "100, 20, 30, 50 doesn't change the look of the pizza, doesn't change the taste … it just changes the time".
- 12-inch pizzas; fine semolina for stretching.

### 2.4 V4 — How to Manage the FERMENTATION Process (2021-05-01) — the master video

Recipe in the description: "Poolish (300ml water-300gr flour, 5gr yeast, 5gr honey) / 100ml water / 10gr salt / 300gr flour". I.e. **600 g poolish + 100 ml water + 300 g flour + 10 g salt → 600 g flour, 400 g water = 66.7% hydration, salt 1.7%, IDY 5 g = 0.83% of flour, poolish = 50% of flour.**

Yeast-quantity rule (verbatim, cleaned): "the yeast never change because the yeast when it melts in the water basically it multiplies the cells in the water so you don't have to put more or less. **Every five liter water the yeast will change, will increase double: so if you're using zero to five liters it's five grams of yeast, 5 liters to 10 liters is 10 grams** … if you're thinking well if I put only five grams it's not gonna rise — yes it's going to rise if you're going to give the right fermentation."

Poolish timing: "when you're going to mix water with yeast basically you have to wait **one hour to two hours depends on the room temperature** to activate the process … then you're gonna put it in the fridge and block it, after **16 to 24 hours** we have the poolish." "You can use the poolish just right away from the fridge."

Poolish % vs time (his words): "the more percentage of poolish you put the faster it will grow … **100% poolish … is gonna grow in one hour but always if the room temperature is really around 80–75 Fahrenheit** [24–27 °C] … today it's hot … **if you're gonna use 30% of poolish the dough will take around two hours to three hours, if you're gonna use 50% it's gonna take around one hour and a half, two hours** … the temperature is always different for everybody in the world so that's why I cannot calculate exactly the time … what I can tell you is that you can control the fermentation timing."

Hot-weather handling (kitchen "about 80 [°F] today, so it's hot"):
- "**I like to use the cold water … using the cold water and the cold poolish will slow down the fermentation process and your dough is not gonna explode.**"
- "if you touch it the dough it's cold that's a good sign … **never use warm water, warm water will speed up the process of fermentation** and trust me you don't want to speed up."
- Rest 15–20 min covered, reshape ball, oil, rest ~15 min more, then ball. "**400 ml of water → four balls, 500 ml of water → five balls**, this is how you calculate the dough balls" (≈ 100 ml water ≈ 250 g ball at his ratios).
- "right now it's really hot so what we do is we wait around **15 to 20 minutes** [after balling] then put it in the fridge; the fridge will slow down the fermentation because it's already fermented, **but if you put the dough too early it will block it and that is going to be a mess, it's not going to grow**. So wait 15–20 minutes if it's hot around 80 then block it in the fridge."
- Cold room: "put it in the warmer place of the room or put it in the oven with the light on (oven off) and keep it there for about one hour to two hours."
- Why poolish is "fast": "the fermentation process is already active … we just can wait one hour, two hours because … the dough needs to relax."

Point of fermentation and refrigeration timing (80 °F):
- At **30 min** at room temp after balling: "it grew one time and a quarter … **this is the point that the dough needs to go in the fridge if you want to use it in about two, three hours or tonight** … tonight 20 minutes before making the pizza you take it out."
- If using within the hour: "keep it outside … this dough is gonna be good for one hour to two hours; **after two hours it's gonna become overproof. If the dough will become overproof you go ahead and re-ball it** and you're gonna be good."
- At **1 h 30 min at 80 °F**: "this is the right point of fermentation … double up the sides and the dough is attached to each other … when you touch it it needs to be full of air and light … the little bubbles on top of the dough, when you press you see this bubble … that means the dough is ready."

## 3. Contradictions between sources and how to resolve them

1. **Salt in the Next Level dough: 25 g (spoken, V1) vs 30 g (Napo, cooked.wiki, BigOven).** Resolve: it is 2.5–3.0% of flour either way; Dawood already uses 3.5% for stickiness control with maida — keep 3.0–3.5%, do not go below 2.5%.

2. **Poolish water temperature: cold (V2, V4, V5, V6, Napo) vs lukewarm (copymethat/cooked write-up of V12) vs unstated (V1, V3).** Every Vito video filmed in warm conditions says cold. Resolve for Islamabad: **cold (fridge, 5–10 °C) water for the poolish whenever the kitchen is above ~24 °C**, and ice-cold water for the final dough (V4, V5). Lukewarm only in winter kitchens (12–18 °C).

3. **Poolish counter time: 1 h (V1, V2, V3, V8) vs 1–2 h "depends on room temperature" (V4, V6) vs 15 min uncovered + 1 h (LifeByMikeG class).** Resolve: Vito's 5 g / 300 g is a big dose; the counter hour is only to "activate". In a 28–34 °C kitchen 1 h is the maximum; at 20 °C or below allow 2 h. The context-handoff's "1.5–2 h" for 28 °C is on the long side of Vito's guidance; recommend 45–60 min at ≥28 °C.

4. **Yeast scaling: Vito's "5 g for 0–5 L water, 10 g for 5–10 L" (V4) vs baker's-percentage practice (TFL, pizzamaking: 0.1–0.3% IDY of flour for 12–24 h).** Vito's rule is not a scaling rule at all — he uses the same 5 g whether the batch is 4 balls (V4) or 14 balls (V2, 1 L water). It works for him because (a) the poolish gets only 1 h warm then 16–24 h at 3–4 °C, (b) honey/sugar exhaustion caps activity, (c) he then adds only 1–2 h warm time. For the engine: **express yeast as a percentage of poolish flour and let time/temperature set it**; treat 5 g / 300 g (1.7% of poolish flour) as an upper bound that only makes sense with an immediate cold hold, and treat 0.1–0.3% as the room-temperature-poolish regime.

5. **Extra yeast in the final dough: none (V1, V3, V4, V12) vs 5 g extra (V2, "100% poolish"), 3 g extra (V7, V8 20% biga), 1 g extra (V8 100% biga).** Resolve: Vito adds extra yeast only when the final dough goes into a second 24 h cold bulk or when a low pre-ferment % must proof in a few hours. With a same-day ball proof after an overnight poolish (context-handoff arrangement B) add none.

6. **Poolish share of total flour: 30% (V1, Napo), 50% (V4), 20–100% (V3, V8 experiments), 69% (V2), 19% (V12), 23% (V6).** Vito's own statement resolves it: the % only changes speed, not taste (V3), and hot day → lower %, cold day → higher % (V3, V4). The engine should choose the poolish % from room temperature and the available ball-proof window, not fix it at 25%.

7. **Ball proof after a cold bulk: 2 h (V1), 1–2 h (V2, V7), 2–4 h (cooked.wiki), 4–6 h (Jordo's calculator), "until doubled" (V5, V6).** Jordo's 4–6 h and its "poolish room temp 16–20 h" are the calculator author's own choices and contradict every Vito video (Vito's poolish is 1 h warm + 16–24 h cold). Resolve: use Vito's observed numbers (doubling in ~1.5 h at 27 °C with 50% poolish; ~2 h at ~24 °C after a cold bulk; 3–4 h at 24 °C with 20% poolish) and let the engine interpolate; never rely on the calculator's timings.

8. **How long balls keep in the fridge: 2–3 days, max 4 (V5, 2026) vs "up to 5 days" (copymethat write-up of V12) vs 7 days still usable (V11, 2019) vs context-handoff "another 18–24 h".** Resolve: V5 is Vito's current professional practice — **plan on 2–3 days, 4 max**; V11 shows the dough does not turn acid at 7 days at fridge temperature, so a 3-day-old ball is safe. For weak maida (less gluten to hold gas, faster enzymatic slackening) cap at 48 h and warn beyond that.

9. **When to refrigerate balls in hot weather: 15–20 min after balling (V4, "if you put the dough too early it will block it") vs after they have doubled (V5) vs at 1.25× after 30 min (V4, same video).** These are three different goals: (V4-15 min) balls that will proof later today/tonight; (V4-30 min) balls for "2–4 hours or tonight"; (V5) balls for tomorrow/day after. Resolve in the engine by the hours until bake: <1.5 h → leave out; 2–8 h → fridge at ~1.25× (about 20–30 min after balling at 28 °C); >8 h → fridge immediately after balling with 15–20 min rest, and use a lower poolish % or less yeast.

10. **Biga hydration in V7 (captions "1 L water per 1 kg flour") vs V8 and V9 (50%).** Captions in V7 are unreliable (Italian/English mix). Resolve: Vito's biga = 50% hydration, 0.3–0.5% IDY, 30–60 min warm, 24–48 h cold.

11. **Hydration: 70% (V1), 69% (V2, V3 rule, V5), 66.7% (V4), 64.5% (V12), 61.5% (V6 same-day), 75% (V9 with "super strong" flour).** All of these are with strong Italian 00 or Manitoba. For weak maida + 20% fine atta: the atta absorbs more water but the maida gluten cannot hold 70%. Keep the context-handoff's 65% default; allow 68–70% only for the long cold-bulk arrangement (C) where the gluten has time to relax and the dough is handled cold.

12. **"Use poolish straight from the fridge" (V4, V5) vs "wait 30 min" (V1, Napo) vs 30–45 min (copymethat).** Both are Vito. In a 30 °C kitchen use it cold — it is one of his two heat controls (cold water + cold poolish, V4).

13. **When to freeze: after the balls reach the point of fermentation, thaw overnight in the fridge + 1 h out (V10, 2020) vs freeze immediately after balling with no proof, thaw and proof 4–6 h at room temperature (V17, 2022).** V17 is the newer "next level" method and is the safer one for weak maida: a ball frozen un-proofed keeps all its gas-holding capacity for a proper proof after thawing, whereas a maida ball frozen at full proof thaws slack. Engine: freeze un-proofed balls (V17); thaw = fridge overnight (V10) then room temp until doubled: ~2.5–3 h at 28 °C, 4–6 h at 20–24 °C (V17 says 4–6 h at an unstated warm room, 5 h observed).

14. **Bulk rest before balling: 15–20 min (V1, V2, V4), 20 min (V5), 30 + 30 min (V17), 1 h (V15, cooked.wiki of V12), 4 h at room temp before refrigerating (V5, next-day balls).** All consistent with one rule: the warm bulk time plus the warm ball time together should reach "doubled" at bake time; Vito shifts time between the two stages freely. Engine: fixed 20 min bench rest, all remaining warm time goes to the balls (better shape retention for weak maida).

15. **Fridge life of balls: 2–3 days max 4 (V5, poolish dough); 4–5 days (V16, 0.5% IDY direct dough); 5 days (copymethat/V12); 7 days "the max" but already heavy and acidic (V15); 7 days preferred (V11, 2019).** Resolve: Vito's 2024–2026 statements (V5, V15, V16) converge on 3–5 days with quality falling after day 3; engine default 3 days, hard stop 5, and for maida warn after 48 h.

16. **Ball-proof calibration points for straight vs pre-fermented dough:** 0.5% IDY direct dough, 280 g balls, doubled at 3.5 h (V16, ~21 °C); no-added-yeast re-mixed dough, 4 h (V14, ~21 °C); poolish dough 24% pre-fermented flour, 1 h bulk + 2 h balls (V15, ~22 °C); poolish dough 50% pre-fermented, 1.5 h at 27 °C (V4). These four points are mutually consistent with the table in §4.2.

## 4. Recommendations for the deterministic engine

Numbers below are Vito's, with the weak-maida corrections stated explicitly. "IDY" = Saf-Instant.

### 4.1 Fixed formula rules (independent of temperature)
- Flour-per-water rule (V3): total flour = total water ÷ hydration. Vito's default 145 g flour per 100 ml water (69%). For maida use hydration 0.65 by default (user input 0.62–0.70).
- Ball count rule (V4): ~100 ml total water per ~250 g ball. For 280 g balls: total dough mass = n × 280 × 1.02 (scrap), flour = mass ÷ (1 + hyd + 0.035 + 0.05), water = flour × hyd.
- Salt: 3.0–3.5% of total flour (Vito 2.4–3.4%; context-handoff 3.5%). Add salt after the first 3–4 min of mixing (V5).
- Oil: 1% (V1, wood oven) to 3% (V2, electric oven) of flour; the CasaKoa is a 450–500 °C oven, so **1–2%** (10–20 g/kg), not the 5% in the handoff, unless burning is not an issue.
- Honey in poolish: 5 g per 300 g poolish flour (1.7%); 10 g in the 2 h fast poolish (V6).
- Poolish hydration always 100%. Pre-ferment options: poolish (all Neapolitan cases) or biga 50% hydration (V8/V9) — the engine can offer biga as an alternative for the ≥48 h plan only.

Engine rule (linear in poolish %, Q10-style in temperature, calibrated on the rows above):
- t_ready(28 °C, P) ≈ 1.0 h at P=100%, 1.5 h at 50%, 2.5 h at 30%, 3.5 h at 20%, ~5 h at 10%.
- For each 3 °C cooler multiply by 1.3; for each 3 °C warmer divide by 1.3 (Vito never states a Q10; this factor reproduces his 24 °C vs 27 °C observations to within 20 min).
- **Weak maida correction: multiply the safe window by 0.8 and treat "ready" as 1.7–1.8× volume rather than Vito's 2× — maida balls collapse sooner; the context-handoff already saw 3–3.5 h at 28 °C for a 25% poolish, which matches this table (3.5 h × 0.9).**
- Choose P so that t_ready equals the user's available window (bake time − mix time − 40 min for rest/balling), clamped to 20–50%. Vito's explicit direction: hot day → lower P; cold day → higher P (V3, V4). In a 30–34 °C kitchen, P = 20–25% and refrigerate balls at 1.25× if the window is > 2 h.

Yeast in the poolish (IDY, % of poolish flour):
- Vito: 5 g / 300 g = 1.7%, only valid with ≤1 h warm then 16–24 h fridge (V1–V5). At 28–34 °C with a 3–6 °C home fridge that can drift to 7–8 °C, this is the overproof risk the handoff worried about.
- Recommend: **1.0% (3 g/300 g) when kitchen ≥ 28 °C, 1.3% (4 g) at 22–27 °C, 1.7% (5 g) below 22 °C** for the 16–24 h fridge poolish; poolish counter time 45–60 min at ≥28 °C, 60–90 min at 22–27 °C, 2 h below 22 °C (V4: "1–2 h depends on room temperature").
- Room-temperature-only poolish (no fridge, 8–12 h overnight): 0.1–0.3% IDY of poolish flour (TFL BirdieandBear/Albacore at 20 °C); at 26 °C night temperature use 0.1%.
- Same-day fast poolish (V6): 10 g IDY / 300 g (3.3%) + 10 g honey, 1–2 h until doubled at ~24 °C; at 30 °C reduce to 6–7 g and expect ~1 h.
- Extra IDY in the final dough: 0 for arrangements A and B; for a second 16–24 h cold bulk (arrangement C) Vito adds 0.35% (5 g/1450 g, V2) — for maida use 0.15–0.2% or none, since a maida bulk at 6–8 °C for 24 h is already at its limit.

### 4.3 Timelines the engine should generate (clock-time steps)
Arrangement B (Vito V1/V4 hybrid, recommended default): poolish (cold water) → 45–60 min counter → fridge 16–24 h → next day poolish used cold (V4/V5) → mix with ice-cold water, salt after 3–4 min, ≤20 min total mixing (V5) → rest 20 min → ball 280 g → proof per 4.2 table → bake.
Arrangement A (same day, ≤5 h): V6 fast poolish 1–2 h → mix → 20 min rest → balls 2–3 h (V6) → bake; 61–65% hydration; extra oil only if home oven.
Arrangement C (48 h "Next Level", events with lots of balls): V1: mix → 15–20 min rest → fold → oiled sealed box → fridge 16–24 h → out 30–60 min → ball → 1–2 h at ≤24 °C, ~1 h at ≥28 °C (V1: "if it's hot it may take also only one hour") → bake. Second-day balls: V5 — after balling let them reach ~1.25–1.5× at room temp (20–40 min at 28 °C) then fridge; take out 30 min before use (V5) — for cold, tight maida balls the handoff's 2–3 h warm-up applies, so the engine should schedule "out of fridge" at bake − 2.5 h at 28 °C and bake − 3.5 h at 20 °C.

### 4.4 Leftover balls
- Eaten later today (2–8 h): fridge at 1.25× (V4), out 20–30 min before at 28 °C (V4/V5), 60 min at 20 °C.
- Tomorrow or the day after: fridge after 15–20 min rest (V4) or once ~doubled (V5); max 2–3 days, 4 absolute (V5); for maida cap at 48 h. Out of the fridge 30 min (V5) — engine: 45 min at ≥28 °C, 90 min at ≤22 °C — and pop large surface bubbles.
- Later than 3 days: freeze at the point of fermentation (V10) sealed flat; 2–3 months; thaw in the fridge overnight on a semolina-dusted tray, out 1 h before (V10); keep the thawed pizza ≤10 inches (V10).
- Overproofed ball (large bubbles, slack, but no acid smell): re-ball and wait 30–60 min (V4, V8); acid smell → discard/bread (V5, V8).

### 4.5 Readiness tests to print in the step list
- Poolish ready: bubbly, may have just started to flatten (V1, V3 — flat is normal after 16 h cold). Dead/no bubbles → yeast problem (V5).
- Balls ready: doubled, touching each other in the box, small bubbles under the surface when pressed, feel light/full of air (V4). Weak maida: stop at ~1.75×.
- Too far: many large bubbles on top → fridge now or re-ball (V5, V4).

### 4.6 Oven
V5 pizzeria oven: top 370–400 °C, floor ~300 °C, 90 s without moving the pizza. This is lower on the floor than the handoff's 380–400 °C floor target; with high-starch maida (browns fast), aim for **floor 350–380 °C, dome 430–470 °C**, turn at 40–45 s.
